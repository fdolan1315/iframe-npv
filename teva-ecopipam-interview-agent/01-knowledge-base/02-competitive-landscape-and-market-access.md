# Knowledge Base — Competitive Landscape & Market Access

> **Purpose:** what ecopipam competes against (drugs, devices, and non-treatment), the pipeline, and the payer/access reality of a pediatric orphan launch. Reference doc for the agents and candidate.
> **Compiled July 2026.** Figures corroborated across multiple sources; primary-source confirmation advised for any customer-facing use.

---

## 1. The competition is not mainly another drug

The single most important framing for the interview: **ecopipam's real competition at launch is therapeutic conservatism and cheap generics, not a branded rival.** In priority order:

1. **"Watchful waiting" / non-treatment** — the largest de facto competitor. Most tics are mild, wax-and-wane, and improve with age, so the majority are never medicated. The true pharmacotherapy market is the **~10–30% with moderate-to-severe, impairing, or refractory tics.**
2. **CBIT (behavioral therapy)** — the guideline-credentialed **first-line gatekeeper**, benign safety profile, but only **~38–52% respond** and access is throttled by a documented therapist shortage.
3. **Off-label alpha-2 agonists (guanfacine, clonidine)** — the real-world first-line *pharmacotherapy*, cheap generics, especially for the very common TS+ADHD patient. **Ecopipam's stiffest practical drug competition** for milder tics.
4. **The 3 FDA-approved antipsychotics** — the incumbents ecopipam is built to displace, led by generic **aripiprazole**.
5. **Off-label VMAT2 inhibitors** — largely irrelevant; the modern class **failed** pediatric TS.
6. **Devices (Neupulse wristband; DBS)** — emerging/niche.

## 2. Approved & off-label drugs — the incumbents

**FDA-approved antipsychotics (all D2 blockers; all now generic):**
- **Haloperidol (Haldol)** — approved 1969 (≥3y). Effective but poorly tolerated (sedation, weight gain, parkinsonism, TD). Now largely third-line. Threat: **low**.
- **Pimozide (Orap)** — approved 1984 (≥12y). Signature liability **QT prolongation**; ECG monitoring; multiple contraindications. Threat: **low**.
- **Aripiprazole (Abilify)** — approved for pediatric TS **Dec 2014 (6–18y)**, the most recent TS approval before ecopipam and the most-used. Partial D2/5-HT1A agonist, best-tolerated of the three, but still **+1.5 kg mean weight gain** (vs +0.4 kg placebo) in pediatric TS trials, plus akathisia/EPS/TD class risk. Now **generic, ~$10/month**. Threat: **moderate–high** (main branded-equivalent, but the exact liabilities ecopipam avoids).

**Off-label alpha-2 adrenergic agonists (ADHD-approved; first-line for mild-moderate tics):**
- **Guanfacine ER (Intuniv)** and **clonidine ER (Kapvay)** — reduce **both tics and ADHD** in comorbid patients; AAN Level B. Far milder than antipsychotics (no EPS/TD/metabolic) but cause sedation, hypotension/bradycardia, rebound HTN; require HR/BP monitoring and taper. **Generic and cheap** (guanfacine ~$16/mo, clonidine ER ~$25/mo). Threat: **high** for first-line/milder tics. Ecopipam's wedge: **moderate-to-severe tics and alpha-2 failures**, where today's alternative is a D2 antipsychotic.

**Off-label VMAT2 inhibitors (dopamine-depleters) — the failed challengers:**
- **Deutetrabenazine (Austedo — Teva's own)** **failed both pivotal pediatric TS trials** (ARTISTS 1: YGTSS-TTS diff −0.7, P=0.69; ARTISTS 2: −0.8, P=0.60) → never got a TS indication.
- **Valbenazine (Ingrezza — Neurocrine)** **failed** its serial TS program (T-Forward, T-Force GREEN, Phase 2b **T-Force GOLD**) → program wound down.
- **Tetrabenazine (Xenazine)** — old, off-label refractory use; depression/suicidality boxed warning.
- **Read:** the modern VMAT2 class failed controlled pediatric TS — no credible on-label VMAT2 competitor. Ecopipam's clean Phase 3 win is exactly what deuterabenazine and valbenazine could not deliver.

## 3. The Teva internal portfolio dynamic (Austedo vs ecopipam) — complementary, not cannibalistic

A likely interview question. The evidence points to **complementary**:
- Austedo is a blockbuster (**~$1.6B in 2024**) — but on **adult tardive dyskinesia and Huntington's chorea**, with essentially **no overlap** with pediatric TS.
- Austedo **has no TS indication and failed the trials designed to earn one** (ARTISTS). Teva cannot promote it for tics.
- Ecopipam fills the **gap Austedo left**, reusing the **same neuroscience/movement-disorder commercial infrastructure and prescriber relationships** (movement-disorder neurologists, child neurologists/psychiatrists). Minimal cannibalization; largely additive. The main internal tension is **commercial focus / share-of-voice**, not revenue overlap. Strategically, Teva turns a prior TS failure into a second, differentiated shot at the disease.

## 4. Pipeline — thin, not crowded

Ecopipam is **the only TS asset with positive Phase 3 data and an active NDA.** Everything else is Phase 2 or earlier, failed, or off-label:
- **PDE10A inhibitors** — the most credible *novel-mechanism* challengers, a full phase behind: **gemlapodect (Noema)** at Phase 2b (readout unconfirmed as of mid-2026); **balipodect (Axsome, ex-Takeda)** with TS as a secondary priority behind schizophrenia; **EM-221** preclinical/early. None could realistically file before ~2028–2030.
- **Cannabinoids** — **SciSparc SCI-110** (THC+PEA) at Phase 2b (adults), modest ~21% Phase 2a effect; the class has a history of primary-endpoint misses (CANNA-TICS/nabiximols). Uncertain regulatory path.
- **Conclusion:** ecopipam is very likely the **only genuinely new mechanism approved for TS this decade** — D1 antagonism would be the first mechanistically novel TS approval in >50 years. The near-term threat is entrenched cheap off-label options and devices, not pipeline drugs.

## 5. Non-drug competition

- **CBIT / HRT** — first-line per AAN/European/Canadian bodies; benign; **~38–52% respond**; access throttled by therapist shortage (TAA's TS-BTI trainings are the acknowledged fix). Digital CBIT (TicHelper, iCBIT, ONLINE-TICS) broadens access but doesn't close the response gap.
- **Neupulse (median-nerve stimulation wristband)** — the emerging non-drug challenger to watch. University of Nottingham spin-out; rhythmic 10 Hz median-nerve stimulation, 10 min/day. Sham-controlled RCT (n=135, ages 12+): **~35% YGTSS-TTS reduction / 59% responders** in the active arm (note: the pre-registered *primary* endpoint was tic frequency; the 35% figure is the secondary severity measure). **UK pre-orders opened Oct 2025** (£500 + £20/mo), delivery ~June 2026; **US/EU pending — currently sold as a "wellness product" with no CE/FDA clearance.** Ecopipam's differentiators: regulatory status, efficacy magnitude, and no daily-wear adherence burden. *(Note: the "FirstDVR" name in early notes doesn't correspond to a real device — the intended reference is Neupulse.)*
- **Deep brain stimulation (DBS)** — invasive, adult, reserved for ≥3-drug-refractory extreme (international registry n=185, YGTSS 75→41 at 1 yr, **35% adverse-event rate**). Not a volume competitor; the refractory ceiling.

## 6. Market access — the pediatric orphan reality

**This is a bigger interview topic than most candidates expect. Master it.**

**Pricing:** ecopipam will be a **premium branded orphan drug entering a penny-generic category** — the core access tension. Comparable branded CNS/orphan launches: Austedo (~$90k/yr historically; XR ~$28k–$85k/yr by strength), Ingrezza (~$70k–$113k/yr), pediatric orphan CNS (Epidiolex $32.5k, Fintepla ~$96k). Plausible ecopipam WAC band **~$40k–$100k+/yr**, with weight-based pediatric dosing rising as children grow. Expect ICER-style value pushback given cheap generic comparators. Sell-side (Jefferies) pegs **peak US sales up to ~$1B**, likely positioned **refractory/post-antipsychotic** where pricing power is greatest. Teva declined to give its own peak-sales forecast.

**Payer mix — Medicaid/CHIP heavy:** ~**49% of US children (~37M)** are on Medicaid/CHIP; for a pediatric launch, roughly **half the addressable base** sits there. Consequences: statutory **23.1% basic rebate** (or AMP−Best Price), **uncapped inflation (CPI) penalties** (the 100%-of-AMP rebate cap was removed Jan 1, 2024), Best-Price linkage, and state **PDL/prior-authorization** gating. **Access is won or lost state Medicaid program by state Medicaid program** — high-Medicaid Eastern states (LA 63%, KY 59%, NY 59%, NC 57%) matter most. *(State detail in doc 03.)*

**Prior authorization & step therapy — high:** expect payers to require **documented failure/intolerance of ≥1–2 generics** (an alpha-2 agonist ± a generic antipsychotic) plus YGTSS documentation and specialist prescribing, with PA renewals tied to demonstrated response. Precedent: Intuniv faced step-therapy through generic clonidine/guanfacine. Counter-argument: novel D1 mechanism + no on-label competitor argue against rigid step-through.

**Distribution:** likely **specialty/limited-distribution** despite the oral formulation (mirroring Ingrezza/Austedo) — home delivery, benefits investigation, copay coordination, adherence monitoring.

**Hub / patient support (Teva's existing muscle):** Teva's **Shared Solutions** hub and **Teva Cares Foundation** port directly over — $0 commercial copay cards, free-drug PAP for uninsured/underinsured, and **PA bridge/quick-start** fills (Austedo model: up to 30-day supply, up to 3 fills). **Critical AKS constraint:** copay cards **cannot** be used by Medicaid/Medicare/TRICARE patients — so the ~half of patients on government insurance need the **independent charitable-foundation** channel instead. Deep copay buy-downs also risk lowering Best Price and inflating Medicaid rebates, so programs are structured carefully.

**Orphan-drug stack:** **7-year orphan market exclusivity** on approval, 25% orphan tax credit, PDUFA fee waiver. **Rare Pediatric Disease designation / Priority Review Voucher** is *possible upside* (PRVs recently sold for ~$150–158M; program runs through Sept 30, 2029) but **RPD was not confirmed in public sources — treat as upside, not fact.**

## 7. Market size (context)
- TS **treatment market ~$2.0–2.7B** today, ~5–7% CAGR — but that base is mostly generic/off-label; the branded on-label opportunity ecopipam creates is largely **incremental**.
- Comparable branded movement-disorder launches reached blockbuster in **~4–5 years** (Ingrezza: $117M→$1.1B in ~4 yrs; Austedo: <$100M→~$1B in ~5 yrs) — and Teva already owns the infrastructure that ramped Austedo, a real launch-execution advantage.

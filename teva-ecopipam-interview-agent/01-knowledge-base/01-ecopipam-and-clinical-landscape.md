# Knowledge Base — Ecopipam & the Tourette Syndrome Clinical Landscape

> **Purpose:** the clinical foundation — the disease, how it's treated, the ecopipam data, the patient journey, and objection-handling. Reference doc for the interview/coach agents and the candidate.
> **Compiled July 2026.** Efficacy/safety figures corroborated across the JAMA Neurology Phase 3 paper (PMID 42189524), the Phase 2b Pediatrics paper, and Teva/Emalex disclosures.

---

## 1. Tourette syndrome — clinical overview

**Definition.** A childhood-onset neurodevelopmental movement disorder defined by chronic **motor and vocal (phonic) tics** — sudden, rapid, recurrent, non-rhythmic movements or vocalizations.

**DSM-5-TR criteria (Tourette's Disorder):** both **multiple motor tics AND ≥1 vocal tic** at some time (not necessarily concurrently); tics persist **>1 year**; **onset before age 18**; not due to a substance or other medical condition.
- *Persistent (Chronic) Motor OR Vocal Tic Disorder* = only one type for >1 year. *Provisional Tic Disorder* = tics <1 year.

**Tic types:** simple motor (eye blinking, head jerks, shrugs), complex motor (touching, copropraxia, echopraxia), simple vocal (sniffing, throat clearing, grunting), complex vocal (words, echolalia, palilalia, coprolalia). **Coprolalia is present in only ~10–20%**, contrary to public perception. Many patients report a **premonitory urge** — the target of behavioral therapy.

**Onset & natural history:** tics begin ~**age 4–6**, worst-ever severity ~**age 10–12**, then classic **waxing-and-waning**, worsened by stress/excitement/fatigue. Prognosis is favorable: **~75% substantially improve by adulthood and >⅓ become essentially tic-free**; a minority have persistent impairing tics.

**Epidemiology (US):** ~**0.3% diagnosed** (~174,000 children ages 3–17) / ~**0.6% true prevalence** — about **half are undiagnosed**. Boys **~3:1** (up to 4:1 in clinical series). *(Full epi detail in doc 03.)*

**Severity measurement — YGTSS (Yale Global Tic Severity Scale):** the gold-standard clinician interview and the primary-endpoint instrument in modern tic trials. Total Motor (0–25) + Total Phonic (0–25) = **Total Tic Score / TTS (0–50)**; plus Impairment (0–50); Global Severity (0–100). Common trial responder thresholds: ≥25% or ≥50% TTS reduction.

## 2. Comorbidities — why the "whole patient" matters

TS is rarely isolated. **~86% have ≥1 psychiatric comorbidity; only ~10–15% have "pure" TS.**
- **ADHD ~38–50%** (often precedes tics; frequently more impairing than the tics).
- **OCD ~50%** in clinical cohorts (symmetry, "just-right" phenomena).
- **Anxiety ~53%; Depression ~36%.**

**Why it matters commercially:**
1. **Impairment often comes from the comorbidity, not the tic** — the tic-drug conversation happens in a crowded context.
2. **"Who owns the patient" is fragmented** — ADHD/OCD/anxiety pull toward child psychiatry / developmental-behavioral pediatrics; the tic disorder pulls toward pediatric neurology / movement disorders. Many patients are co-managed.
3. **Mechanism logic** — alpha-2 agonists (guanfacine/clonidine) are attractive because they treat **both tics and ADHD**. Ecopipam must be positioned to **complement, not compete with**, comorbidity management.

## 3. Treatment paradigm & the 2019 AAN guideline

Definitive US reference: **2019 AAN practice guideline**, *"Treatment of Tics in People with Tourette Syndrome and Chronic Tic Disorders"* (*Neurology*, May 2019).

**Core principle — treat impairment, not the tic itself.**
- **Watchful waiting** is explicitly recommended for **non-impairing** tics (foundation of therapeutic conservatism).
- **First-line active treatment = CBIT** (Comprehensive Behavioral Intervention for Tics): habit-reversal training + relaxation + function-based intervention. Level B over both other psychosocial interventions and over medication. Pivotal RCTs: **~52.5% of children improved (vs ~18.5% control), NNT=3, ~7.6-point YGTSS drop**; **~38% of adults (vs ~7%)**. No drug-type side effects — but access is limited (see §6).

**Pharmacotherapy ladder:**

| Class | Agents | FDA status for TS | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alpha-2 agonists** | guanfacine, clonidine | Off-label (common first-line drug) | AAN Level B, esp. with comorbid ADHD (treats both). Sedation, hypotension, bradycardia; HR/BP monitoring. |
| **Antipsychotics (D2 blockers)** | haloperidol (1969, ≥3y), pimozide (1984, ≥12y), **aripiprazole (2014, 6–18y)** | **The only 3 FDA-approved TS drugs** | Effective but tolerability-limited. Aripiprazole most-used; all now generic. |
| VMAT2 inhibitors | tetrabenazine, deutetrabenazine, valbenazine | Off-label | Modern agents **failed controlled pediatric TS trials** (see doc 02). |
| Topiramate; DBS | — | Off-label / device | Topiramate modest; DBS for severe refractory adults only. |

**The antipsychotic tolerability problem (central to ecopipam's story):** all 3 approved drugs block **D2** and carry **weight gain/metabolic syndrome, EPS (dystonia, parkinsonism, akathisia), sedation, and tardive dyskinesia** (rare but potentially irreversible — a serious concern when medicating children for years). Pimozide adds QT prolongation. **No new mechanism has been FDA-approved for TS in over a decade** — the white space ecopipam launches into.

## 4. Ecopipam — product specifics

**Mechanism:** first-in-class, selective **dopamine D1 (D1/D5) receptor antagonist**; a benzazepine with little D2 or 5-HT2 affinity. **Explicitly not an antipsychotic** — mechanistically differentiated from every approved TS drug and free of the D2-related liabilities (EPS, TD, prolactin). Rationale: D1-receptor hypersensitivity is hypothesized to drive the repetitive motor behaviors of TS.

**Regulatory:** FDA **Orphan Drug** + **Fast Track**. **NDA submitted by Teva June 18, 2026** for pediatric TS. If approved, the **first new FDA-approved TS treatment in >10 years and the first-ever non-antipsychotic**. Originated at Emalex (which ran the pivotal program); Teva acquired Emalex and is the NDA sponsor. Expanded Access Program authorized Oct 2025 / dosing 2026. *(No Breakthrough Therapy designation was found — confirmed designations are Orphan + Fast Track.)*

**Pivotal Phase 3 — the D1AMOND study (NCT05615220), *JAMA Neurology*, May 26, 2026:**
- **Design:** double-blind, placebo-controlled, **randomized-withdrawal**. **216 participants (167 [77%] pediatric)**, **77 sites / 12 countries**, enrolled Jan 2023–Feb 2025. 12-week open-label titration to target **1.8 mg/kg/day**, then patients with **≥25% YGTSS-TTS improvement at weeks 8 and 12** randomized 1:1 to continue ecopipam or taper to placebo. Primary endpoint: **time to relapse**.
- **Efficacy:** in the open-label lead-in, **73.2% achieved ≥25% TTS improvement (34.7% ≥50%)**. Continued ecopipam cut relapse risk ~50%: **pediatric HR ≈ 0.5 (reported 0.47; 95% CI 0.3–0.8), P=.008**; combined HR ≈0.5, P=.005. Relapse **41.2% on ecopipam vs 67.9% on placebo**. Met primary + secondary endpoints.
- **Safety (the differentiation story):** **no clinically meaningful effect on weight, metabolic parameters, or drug-induced movement disorders** — precisely the domains that limit antipsychotics. **Weight-neutral, no significant metabolic/EPS signals.** Most common AEs (open-label): **somnolence ~11%, anxiety ~10%, headache ~10%, insomnia ~9%, tic ~8%, fatigue ~7%.**
- **Signals to anticipate objections on:** CNS-activation effects — somnolence, insomnia, headache, **anxiety** (notable given high baseline anxiety comorbidity), restlessness/fatigue.

**Supporting evidence:**
- **Phase 2b (*Pediatrics*, Jan 2023):** >150 children; **~30% TTS reduction vs placebo, P=.01**; CGI-TS also improved (P=.03).
- **12-month open-label extension (*Mov Disord Clin Pract*, 2025):** 121 patients; durable tic reduction with **no tachyphylaxis**, improved QoL, **no adverse BMI/cholesterol/HbA1c trends** — directly supports the weight-neutral/metabolically clean positioning. 11.6% discontinued for AEs.

> **Naming caveat:** "D1AMOND" is most precisely the Phase 3 randomized-withdrawal trial (NCT05615220). Some trade coverage loosely applied the name to the earlier Phase 2b. Keep them separate (Phase 2b ≈150 pts, 30% reduction; Phase 3 = 216 pts, relapse-prevention). Confirm exact secondary-endpoint deltas from the JAMA Neurology paper before customer-facing use.

## 5. The patient journey

- **Onset → recognition:** first symptoms most often in preschool (~40%) or early primary school (~36%), usually **motor tics first** (~56%). Early tics mistaken for allergies, vision problems, or behavior.
- **Diagnostic delay & referral churn:** diagnosis commonly lags onset by years (often not formalized until primary/middle school). Parents consult on average **~4 different types of professional before diagnosis** — GP, pediatrician, psychologist, pediatric neurologist, child psychiatrist. Once the *right* specialist is reached, diagnosis is usually quick (TS is a clinical diagnosis — no confirmatory test).
- **Typical pathway:** primary-care pediatrician → developmental-behavioral pediatrician / child psychiatry (where ADHD/OCD/anxiety dominate) → **pediatric neurology / movement-disorder specialist** (confirmation, complex/refractory cases, pharmacologic management).
- **Launch implication:** patients are distributed across pediatrics, psychiatry, and neurology; ~half undiagnosed. Both a **diagnosis-awareness opportunity** and a need to **target the specialists who actually initiate tic-specific pharmacotherapy** (predominantly pediatric neurology, secondarily child psychiatry).

## 6. Clinical objection-handling angles

**Objection 1 — Therapeutic conservatism: "Tics wax and wane and most kids improve by their late teens. Why medicate a child?"**
- Acknowledge the guideline: watchful waiting is correct for *non-impairing* tics; ecopipam is not for those patients.
- Reframe to impairment and the treatment window: peak severity is **age 10–12** — the school years, when tics cause stigma, bullying, physical pain/injury, and academic disruption. "Wait it out" ignores years of impairment during formative development.
- Differentiate on risk-benefit: historical conservatism is partly rational avoidance of **antipsychotic toxicity** in children. A **weight-neutral, non-antipsychotic, EPS-sparing** agent changes that calculus.

**Objection 2 — "CBIT should come first."**
- Agree — don't fight the guideline. Position ecopipam as **complementary**: for patients who can't access CBIT, don't respond adequately, or need faster/adjunctive control.
- Note the ceiling: even in trials **~half of children don't achieve significant CBIT response**, and it requires cognitive maturity and sustained engagement.

**Objection 3 — CBIT access is limited (turn the barrier into an opening).**
- The TAA formally acknowledges a **critical shortage of CBIT-trained therapists**; barriers include too few clinicians, cost, travel, and families never having heard of CBIT. First-line CBIT is frequently unavailable in practice — a gap a well-tolerated oral option can fill (while awaiting CBIT or when it isn't an option). Telehealth/self-guided CBIT (TicHelper, iCBIT) broadens access but doesn't close the response gap.

**Objection 4 (anticipate) — tolerability/AE signals.**
- Meet head-on with the **weight-neutral, no-EPS, no-TD, metabolically clean** profile vs D2 antipsychotics, backed by 12-month extension data. Be transparent about ecopipam's actual AE set (somnolence, insomnia, headache, anxiety, fatigue) and frame it against the far heavier antipsychotic burden. Proactively address **anxiety** given the high baseline comorbidity.

## Quick-reference fact sheet
- **Dx:** DSM-5-TR — multiple motor + ≥1 vocal tic, >1 yr, onset <18. **Onset ~4–6; peak ~10–12; ~75% improve by adulthood.**
- **Prevalence:** ~0.3% diagnosed / ~0.6% total; ~half undiagnosed; boys ~3:1.
- **Severity:** YGTSS (TTS 0–50; Global 0–100).
- **Comorbidity:** ~86% any psychiatric; OCD ~50%, ADHD ~40%, anxiety ~53%, depression ~36%; only ~10–15% "pure."
- **Guideline:** 2019 AAN — watchful waiting; **CBIT first-line**; alpha-2 agonists (off-label, Level B); **only 3 FDA-approved = haloperidol, pimozide, aripiprazole (all D2 antipsychotics).**
- **Ecopipam:** selective **D1 antagonist, non-antipsychotic**; **Phase 3 D1AMOND** (n=216) pediatric relapse **HR ~0.5, P=.008**; **weight-neutral, no metabolic/EPS**; AEs somnolence ~11%, anxiety/headache ~10%, insomnia ~9%. **Orphan + Fast Track; NDA filed June 18, 2026; JAMA Neurology.**
